Botanical research / 02

From resting bulb
to open iris.

Eight visual milestones explain the full cycle. They overlap in nature; these are presentation stages, not a universal day-by-day schedule.

Species correction: Dutch iris is a bulbous plant. For this experience the player places a mature bulb, with its neck upward. The playable sequence runs through first bloom; fading and dormancy are documented for biological context. RHS: Dutch iris profile

01 / PROPOSED PLAYABLE

Dormant bulb

Ovoid bulb with a dry brown tunic and pointed neck. No living root fan during dormancy. Mississippi State: Dutch Iris for the Farmer Florist

Visual direction: Begin with a mature, planting-ready bulb, not a seed. Keep the bulb below the soil.

02 / PROPOSED PLAYABLE

Rooting

Several pale roots emerge from the basal plate on the underside of the bulb. University of Florida: bulb basal plate

Visual direction: Extend individual root tips at unequal rates; keep every root attached to the base.

03 / PROPOSED PLAYABLE

Shoot emergence

A compact green shoot rises through the soil before the leafy plant develops. Mississippi State: Dutch Iris for the Farmer Florist

Visual direction: Show a pointed vegetative spear, not a purple flower bud or a hooked seedling. Rooting and emergence can overlap.

04 / PROPOSED PLAYABLE

Leaf growth

Narrow, smooth strap leaves overlap at the base; they have a much finer outline than broad sunflower leaves. NC State: Dutch iris anatomy

Visual direction: Lengthen blades from their bases with parallel veins and slight bending; no oval seed-leaf pair.

05 / PROPOSED PLAYABLE

Bud and colour tip

A slender closed bud develops on the flower stem. The pencil stage shows a small coloured tip. Mississippi State: Dutch Iris for the Farmer Florist

Visual direction: Raise the stalk, then enlarge the pointed bud within its green sheath. Do not open petals prematurely.

06 / PROPOSED PLAYABLE

Open flower

Three upright standards alternate with three spreading or drooping falls. Dutch iris is beardless; falls may carry yellow markings. NC State: Dutch iris anatomy

Visual direction: Match the supplied blue-violet and pale lavender reference with yellow signals. Open the parts gradually; retain threefold symmetry.

07 / LATER CYCLE

After flowering

Flowers fade before the foliage finishes its season; the leaves remain to support the bulb. RHS: growing bulb irises

Visual direction: Wilt the petals first while keeping leaves green. This is later-cycle research, beyond the first-bloom endpoint.

08 / LATER CYCLE

Return to dormancy

Foliage eventually dies back; daughter bulbs can form. Dutch iris loses its living roots during rest. Mississippi State: Dutch Iris for the Farmer Florist

Visual direction: Show yellow then straw-brown leaves and fading old roots. Offsets and the following season remain a later extension.

Research notes: flowering is seasonal and varies with conditions. RHS describes roughly 60cm flowering height and late-spring/early-summer bloom; these guide proportions, not a game timer. The world / 03

One glass tank.
Different beginnings.

Plant bulbs at different moments. Vary root paths, leaf lean, stalk height, and flower orientation while retaining iris anatomy.

A flat aquarium for soil.

Wide rectangular glass cuboid, with depth about 12% of its width. Open top, clear front, narrow side and bottom panes. Soil sits below the rim. Bulbs are fully buried behind the front pane, with soil above the neck and room below for roots. No standing water.

Depth without losing the roots.

Slightly elevated three-quarter frontal camera, gentle perspective, fine glass edges, restrained reflections and a soft grounding shadow. Roots stay inside the glass against opaque soil with small grain occlusions. Fit view includes the base, root tips and tallest flower.

The same tank proportions apply on desktop and mobile. The shallow root display is a visual abstraction. Maintain naturally slender mature stems and modest bulb size; enlarge the macro view for detail instead of enlarging every bulb. Roots develop during simulation rather than revealing a finished network.

Flower identity

Blue-violet.
A flash of yellow.

Use the supplied image for colour, petal veining and delicacy. It does not establish a named cultivar. Keep the three upright standards, three falls and petal-like style arms readable.

Dutch iris has a true bulb and smooth narrow leaves. It is not the thick surface rhizome and broad foliage of a bearded iris. NC State: Dutch iris anatomy

Soft daylight.

Warm light from upper left; soft contact shadows, subtle leaf and petal transmission. Keep garden background blurred and the active plant sharp. Avoid glowing roots, glossy mud and plastic petals.

One visual standard.

Use one high-quality renderer with no graphics settings. Violet and yellow belong mainly to the flowers; keep ivory, forest and restrained gold in the interface. Soil conditions are automatic; watering stays outside scope.

The interface / 05

The same quiet frame.
A different plant.

A readable clock.

Elapsed time is the shared garden clock; each bulb has its own age. Keep pause/resume, 1×/5×/20×, restart and Fit view. Remove percentage progress and backward scrubbing. Biological timing is illustrative, not exact days.

Small screen, same world

A reachable garden.

Put compact sidebar content above the scene. Maintain at least 420px scene height and 44px touch targets. Fit the full tank and tall flowers with margin. Use zoom for fine root detail.

Proposed endpoint: each plant holds at its first open bloom while other plants continue growing. This is a presentation choice; real flowers subsequently fade. No automatic loop.

Interaction language / 06

Choose a place.
Plant a bulb.

Keep planting separate from camera gestures. These native diagrams define the preview and placed states.

01 / AIM

A point-up preview.

Choose Plant bulb. A subtle gold bulb outline previews a valid buried location behind the front pane. Keep soil above its neck; reject positions outside the tank or overlapping another bulb.

02 / PLACE

A separate beginning.

Click or tap to place a brown papery bulb and select it. Start its age at placement; existing plants continue independently. Remove the preview outline at the planted location.

Individual development

Vary organ timing and root paths. Grow new root segments and leaf lengths rather than scaling a complete plant.

Automatic conditions

Assume suitable moisture, light and bulb preparation. No watering tool, moisture meter or drying penalty.

Inspection

Wheel/pinch zoom; right-drag or two-finger pan. Fit view restores the angled full-tank composition.

Continuous opening

Leaves lengthen; the bud swells; petals unfold around a stable attachment. Never pop a complete flower into view.

Research and handoff

What the next build
must preserve.

Research informs anatomy. The glass display, timing, variation rules and bloom hold are design decisions, not a validated botanical model.

Review anatomy and layout.

Check dormant bulb, root attachment, emergence, leaves, bud and open bloom on desktop/mobile. Verify three standards and three falls, yellow markings, no fuzzy beard, no sunflower seed shell or cotyledons. Keep bulbs and roots embedded, all flowers in frame and controls unobstructed.

Keep later work separate.

The implementation ends at first bloom. Senescence, offset reproduction, seasonal recurrence, watering, graphics selectors and saved progress remain outside it. Implementation details and verification are recorded in plant-simulation.md.
